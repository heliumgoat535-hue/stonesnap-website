Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

How does Stone Snap identify rocks and minerals?

expand_more

Stone Snap uses Google Gemini AI to analyze photos of rocks, minerals, and crystals. When you take a photo, the image is sent to Google's Gemini model which cross-references visual traits like color, luster, crystal structure, and texture against known geological specimens. The AI returns an identification along with chemical composition, Mohs hardness rating, formation history, and common uses.

What rocks, minerals, and crystals can Stone Snap identify?

expand_more

Stone Snap can identify common igneous rocks (granite, basalt, obsidian, pumice), sedimentary rocks (limestone, sandstone, shale), metamorphic rocks (marble, slate, quartzite, gneiss), minerals (quartz, feldspar, mica, calcite, pyrite, amethyst, azurite), and crystals. The AI performs best with clear, well-lit photos showing the specimen's surface texture and color.

Is Stone Snap free to use?

expand_more

Stone Snap is free to download on Android with 5 free rock identifications. After that, Stone Snap Pro is available for $4.99 per month with a 3-day free trial. Pro unlocks unlimited identifications, full access to Rocky AI Chat, the complete Learning Hub with quizzes and flashcards, and all premium features.

Does Stone Snap work offline?

expand_more

Rock identification requires an internet connection because photos are processed by Google Gemini AI on Google's servers. However, your personal rock collection, identification history, quiz scores, and learning progress are all stored locally on your device and accessible offline.

Is Stone Snap safe for kids?

expand_more

Yes. Stone Snap includes a dedicated Kid Mode with simplified language, colorful visuals, and a fun interface designed for young explorers. The app does not require account creation, does not collect personally identifiable information from children, and all data is stored locally on the device.

Who created Stone Snap?

expand_more

Stone Snap was created by Luna, a lifelong rock collector and geology enthusiast. She built the app because she was tired of lugging field guides on hikes and wanted a tool that could identify any specimen on the spot. Stone Snap uses Google Gemini AI to power its identification engine.

The Story Behind the App

Meet Luna

Luna has been collecting rocks since she could walk. Shoe boxes full of quartz, pockets full of gravel her parents didn't appreciate, and a college geology textbook that fell apart from overuse.

She built Stone Snap because the existing options were terrible. Field guides are heavy. Google Lens gives you a Wikipedia link. She wanted something that could look at a rock and actually tell you what it is — the chemistry, the hardness, where it forms, what it's used for. So she plugged into Google's Gemini AI and built the tool herself.

"I just wanted an app that could do what my geology professor did — look at a rock and rattle off everything about it."

— Luna, Creator of Stone Snap
